# 🎯 Theory of Events

## Introduction

A **Sample Space** contains every possible outcome of a random experiment. However, in practice, we are usually interested in only some of those outcomes.

For example, when rolling a die, we may want the probability of getting an even number rather than any number.

This collection of desired outcomes is called an **Event**.

An event is simply a subset of the sample space.

---

# Event Definition

If the sample space is

$$
S
$$

then an event is any subset

$$
E \subseteq S
$$

An event may contain:

- One outcome
- Several outcomes
- Every outcome
- No outcomes

---

# Types of Events

## Simple Event

Contains exactly one outcome.

Example:

Rolling a 6.

---

## Compound Event

Contains two or more outcomes.

Example:

Rolling an even number.

---

## Certain Event

Occurs with probability

$$
1
$$

It is the entire sample space.

---

## Impossible Event

Contains no outcomes.

Probability is

$$
0
$$

---

# Event Operations

Using set theory, we define:

- Union
- Intersection
- Complement
- Difference

These operations help combine and compare events.

---

# Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events are mutually exclusive if they cannot occur together.

Their intersection is empty.

---

# Exhaustive Events

A collection of events is exhaustive if together they cover the entire sample space.

---

# Why Events Matter

Events allow us to compute probabilities, model uncertainty, and build probabilistic AI systems.

Every classification, prediction, and decision made by an AI model corresponds to one or more events within a sample space.

---

# Summary

Events are subsets of sample spaces that describe outcomes of interest. Understanding events and their relationships is essential for probability, statistics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence.
